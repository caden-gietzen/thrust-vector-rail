# Servo Model Selection

This document records the candidate model comparison and the rationale behind the accepted
servo model. The identified parameters and validation results are in
[results.md](results.md); the identification procedure is in [procedure.md](procedure.md).
All numbers here are for the **upgraded servo** (swapped 2026-06-29), nominal **±15° operating
rung** of the amplitude-ladder PRPS campaign.

## 1. What is being selected

Two related choices:

1. **Instantaneous map vs. explicit actuator dynamics** — can the servo be treated as a static
   command-to-angle gain, or must it enter the rail plant as a dynamic state?
2. **Model structure** — if dynamic, which low-order transfer-function structure, and is a single
   structure enough or should model-order uncertainty be carried into the V&V sweep?

## 2. Instantaneous map vs. explicit dynamics

The servo **cannot** be treated as an instantaneous map. The identified lag + delay
($\tau_\theta + L_\theta \approx 30.5$ ms) contributes $\sim 11^\circ$ of phase at 1 Hz and much
more approaching the $\sim 9$ Hz corner. Since the rail loop crosses over below the servo corner,
that phase is inside the loop and must be modelled. The servo therefore enters the plant as a
first-order-plus-delay actuator state,

$$
\dot{\theta} = -\frac{1}{\tau_\theta}\,\theta + \frac{K_\theta}{\tau_\theta}\,u_{servo}(t - L_\theta).
$$

## 3. Candidate structures

Kept deliberately low-order — the goal is the simplest structure that captures the
control-relevant dynamics, not the lowest achievable fit error.

| Structure | Form | Rationale to consider |
|---|---|---|
| first-order | $\dfrac{K}{1+\tau s}$ | dominant lag, delay negligible |
| **first-order + delay (FOPD)** | $\dfrac{K}{1+\tau s}\,e^{-Ls}$ | dominant lag **and** transport delay |
| second-order lag | $\dfrac{K}{(1+\tau_1 s)(1+\tau_2 s)}$ | a second physical pole, no delay |
| second-order lag + delay | $\dfrac{K}{(1+\tau_1 s)(1+\tau_2 s)}\,e^{-Ls}$ | second pole **and** delay |

## 4. Comparison (nominal ±15° rung)

Each structure is fit to the nominal-rung empirical FRF and scored by (a) train weighted FRF
error, (b) magnitude/phase RMSE, and (c) **held-out time-domain VAF** (`lsim` on a reserved
realization). The held-out VAF is the honest arbiter — train error can always be reduced by adding
parameters, so a richer structure only earns its place if it *generalizes* better.

| Structure | # params | $\tau_1/\tau_2$ (ms) | L (ms) | train err | mag RMSE (dB) | held-out VAF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| first-order | 2 | 32.4 / – | 0.0 | 0.1694 | 1.089 | 98.0% |
| **first-order + delay (FOPD)** | 3 | 17.1 / – | 13.4 | 0.0185 | 0.084 | **99.9%** |
| second-order lag | 3 | 15.1 / 15.1 | 0.0 | 0.0650 | 0.487 | 99.7% |
| second-order lag + delay | 4 | 11.4 / 11.4 | 6.9 | **0.0147** | 0.055 | 99.9% |

## 5. Rationale for FOPD

- **first-order (no delay) is rejected.** It cannot represent the transport delay, so it lumps the
  delay into an inflated $\tau = 32.4$ ms and still mis-fits the phase and high-frequency roll-off
  (mag RMSE 1.09 dB, VAF 98.0%). The delay is real, hardware/firmware latency and must be explicit.
- **second-order lag (no delay) is rejected.** It approximates the delay with two equal poles
  ($\tau_1=\tau_2=15.1$ ms) — a Padé-like surrogate — but still trails FOPD in both FRF error and
  VAF. Spending a parameter to fake the delay is strictly worse than modelling it.
- **second-order lag + delay is the lowest *train* error but is not adopted.** It shaves train
  error to 0.0147 (vs FOPD 0.0185), but its **held-out VAF is identical to FOPD (99.9%)** and both
  sit at the sub-0.1 dB measurement floor. Its poles collapse to an **equal pair**
  ($\tau_1=\tau_2=11.4$ ms) with a *reduced* delay (6.9 ms): it is using a repeated pole to
  Padé-approximate part of the same transport delay, not capturing a distinct second dynamic. The
  extra parameter buys a floor-level train-error reduction that does not generalize.
- **FOPD is accepted.** It is the simplest structure that captures the two real effects (a single
  dominant lag and the transport delay), it fits to the measurement floor, and it validates at
  VAF 99.9% on held-out data.

## 6. Structural (model-order) uncertainty — not carried into the sweep

We explicitly checked whether a competing structure is close enough (or better) to warrant carrying
as a **model-mismatch case** in the uncertainty sweep — design on FOPD, test against a richer truth.
The answer is **no**:

- The only structure that beats FOPD on train error (second-order lag + delay) has **identical
  held-out VAF** and is **dynamically equivalent** (its extra pole is a delay reparameterization,
  not a new mode). A mismatch case built from it would be a near-copy of FOPD — a trivially passing,
  low-information test.
- The **real** structural stress on the servo model is its **amplitude-dependence**: across the
  ladder $\tau$ runs 17.1→20.0 ms — a genuine dynamic variation an **order of magnitude larger**
  than the FOPD-vs-higher-order gap. That is already captured as the parametric amplitude window
  (the Stage 2 uncertainty set; see [results.md](results.md)).

**Decision:** FOPD is the single accepted structure; no servo model-order case is added to the
Monte-Carlo sweep. The parametric amplitude window is the operative servo uncertainty. Should a
future closed-loop mismatch ever implicate the unmodelled pole, the second-order-lag-delay
parameters are preserved in the analysis `.mat` and can be promoted to a `p.unc` structural case
then.
