# Friction Identification Results

> **Status: identified, not finalized.** A full residual-based friction campaign has been
> run and analyzed, but the result is **not yet locked in**: the data is still in
> `candidate/` (not triaged to `accepted/`), and two analysis passes disagree on model
> structure and magnitude. This document records the current best estimate and the open
> questions. The model selection and final parameters should be confirmed before friction
> is treated as an "earned" parameter. Method and test design are in
> [procedure.md](procedure.md).

## Summary

Friction is identified by the **residual method**: during open-loop, fixed-angle,
stepped-ESC runs, the rail-direction force is predicted from the identified
[servo](../servo_identification/results.md) and [thrust](../thrust_identification/results.md)
models, and the mismatch against the encoder-derived acceleration is attributed to friction:

$$
\hat{F}_\text{friction}(t) = \hat{F}_\text{thrust}(t)\sin\!\bigl(\hat\theta(t)\bigr) - M\,\hat{\ddot{x}}(t)
$$

The two robust conclusions — stable across both analysis passes — are:

1. **Coulomb friction is large**, $\mu_c$ on the order of **~1 N**.
2. **Directional asymmetry is real and significant** (≈ 29–42% depending on dataset).

Both directly shape the crude stabilizer: this is a low-authority, friction-dominated
system that needs integral action and likely a friction feedforward. See
[docs/rough_truth_model.md §4.3](../../docs/rough_truth_model.md#43-friction).

## Current best estimate (2026-05-29 multi-angle pass)

Latest analysis run — 18 runs from `candidate/`, multi-angle coverage. Generated by
[`analyze_friction_sweep.m`](../../analysis/system_identification/friction_identification/friction_sweep_log/analyze_friction_sweep.m).

**Selected model (pooled): viscous + Coulomb**

$$
\hat{F}_\text{friction}(v) = -\,b\,v \; - \; \mu_c\operatorname{sign}(v)
$$

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---:|---|
| $b$ (viscous) | 2.3154 N·s/m | |
| $\mu_c$ (Coulomb) | 1.0226 N | |
| Pooled RMSE | 0.4360 N | |

**Mass sensitivity** ($M = 0.454 \pm 0.136$ kg, ±0.3 lb):

| Parameter | Range |
|---|---|
| $b$ | [2.2882, 2.3425] N·s/m |
| $\mu_c$ | [1.0210, 1.0242] N |

Coefficients are nearly insensitive to the mass uncertainty band, which is reassuring — the
friction estimate is not an artifact of mass mis-weighing.

**Directional asymmetry**

| Direction | $b$ (N·s/m) | $\mu_c$ (N) | RMSE (N) | $n$ |
|-----------|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| positive | 2.3345 | 1.1335 | 0.2988 | 1238 |
| negative | 2.5246 | 0.8492 | 0.5362 | 894 |

$\Delta b = 7.8\%$, $\Delta\mu_c = 28.7\%$ — above the 20% threshold, so a direction-dependent
friction model is flagged as recommended. **Caveat:** by the test design, positive servo
direction produces positive velocity and negative servo direction produces negative
velocity, so servo direction and motion direction are confounded in this comparison — part
of the measured asymmetry may be servo/geometry-related rather than pure friction.

## Discrepancy with the earlier pass

An earlier (single-angle, pre-redesign) analysis selected **Coulomb-only** with
$\mu_c \approx 0.82$ N, RMSE ≈ 0.24 N, and reported a 42% asymmetry (+0.56 / −0.86 N). Those
numbers previously propagated into the README. They are **superseded** by the multi-angle
pass above but are recorded here because:

- The earlier per-direction Coulomb-only fits had lower residuals (RMSE ≈ 0.23 N) than the
  pooled viscous+Coulomb fit (0.44 N) — the pooled RMSE is inflated by lumping both
  directions and angles together.
- This means the model-selection question (Coulomb-only per-direction vs. pooled
  viscous+Coulomb) is **genuinely open** and depends on how the data is grouped.

For the **rough truth model** this ambiguity does not matter: both agree $\mu_c \sim 1$ N and
asymmetry is significant, so the stabilizer is designed against the high estimate.

## Friction disturbance overbound for V&V

Because the crude stabilizer must be tested against stiction, asymmetry, and intermittent
rail-position effects, a provisional simulation overbound was generated from the current
`candidate/` friction data. The procedure is documented in
[friction_disturbance_overbound.md](friction_disturbance_overbound.md), and the generated
artifacts are saved under
[`plots/system_identification/friction_identification/friction_sweep_log/friction_disturbance_overbound/`](../../plots/system_identification/friction_identification/friction_sweep_log/friction_disturbance_overbound/).

The exported disturbance family has the form:

$$
F_\text{fric}(p, v, r) =
-b_\pm(r)v
-\mu_\pm(r)\operatorname{sign}(v)
-F_\text{spatial}(p, r)
$$

with a separate stiction rule near $v = 0$. The current overbound uses 18 candidate runs and
2132 valid residual samples. It exports low, nominal, high, and asymmetric-worst dynamic
cases; a breakaway range of approximately $0.46$-$1.55$ N; and spatial bump realizations
bounded by the residual-vs-position remainder.

This artifact is for controller robustness testing only. It does not close the open
friction-identification items below, and it should not be treated as an earned friction
model.

## Open items before friction is "earned"

1. **Triage the 2026-05-29 candidate runs** into `accepted/` / `rejected/`.
2. **Settle model selection** — Coulomb-only (per-direction) vs. viscous+Coulomb (pooled);
   decide the grouping that the controller will actually use.
3. **Deconfound** servo direction from motion direction in the asymmetry estimate.
4. **Decide on a direction-dependent model** vs. a robust symmetric bound for the controller.
5. **Quantify stiction/breakaway** explicitly (currently only bracketed by the halt
   classification).

Until these are closed, friction is carried in the [rough truth model](../../docs/rough_truth_model.md)
as a provisional bound ($\mu_c \approx 1$ N, $b \approx 2.3$ N·s/m, ~30% asymmetry), not a
finalized parameter.
